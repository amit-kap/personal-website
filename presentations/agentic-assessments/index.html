Overview

Problem

Large enterprises can have thousands of vendors. Onboarding each one and keeping its assessment up to date demands hundreds of hours of manual work, on an outdated process of countless Excel sheets and endless email threads.

Shift

An agent does the repetitive assessment work. A human reviews exceptions and stays accountable for decisions.

First step

The agent drafts vendor follow-ups for approval, then turns replies into proposed verdict changes.

Success measure

How many vendors one analyst can keep fully assessed, without lowering the quality of each assessment.

Our angle

Others add an assistant to the analyst’s workspace. We build the workspace for the person supervising the agent.

Them

The analyst completes the assessment. AI helps.

Us

The agent completes the assessment. The supervisor decides.

This is the wedge. It is an operating-model choice, not a feature. The screens exist to let a person inspect the agent’s reasoning, step in where judgment is needed, and define where the agent stops.

This slide answers the CISO and CTO question: who is accountable? The person is. Policy is versioned and human-editable, so supervisors guide the agent without code. A correction like “you got AC-04 wrong; save it as a rule” becomes a reviewable policy change.

Technical approach and cost

Agent pipeline

Evidence intake, mapping to controls, drafting follow-ups, parsing replies into proposed verdicts.

Security of the feature

Vendor data handling, tenant isolation, and outbound email sent on the customer’s behalf.

Evaluation as a product surface

Track where the agent is right, where it fails, and where people override it. Show it to customers.

Cost

Model cost per assessment and margin impact: $5–15 per assessment (est.), under 2% of the add-on price. Confirmed in Phase 1.

For the CTO: the hard part is not capability, it is evaluation and safe outbound communication. Phase 1 keeps the agent’s output behind human approval, so the security surface stays small while we measure cost and accuracy.

Earn trust in three phases

Phase 1 · ask today

Follow-up wedge

Agent drafts vendor follow-ups; a person approves; replies become proposed verdicts. 8 people, one quarter (est.).

Gate: ≥ 80% drafts approved unedited, overrides ≤ 10%, after 90 days with design partners.

Phase 2

Fleet and channel

The agent runs whole assessments; people review exceptions in the fleet view and channels.

Gate: analyst hours −50%, zero critical findings missed in audit samples.

Phase 3

Policy and autonomy

Plain-language policy; autonomy expands only where measured results support it.

Gate: per-customer override and accuracy data.

We are only asking for Phase 1 today. Each later phase needs its gate met and comes back to this group. Gate numbers are proposals (est.) to agree on now, so success is defined before we start.
