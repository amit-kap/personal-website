Before a third-party vendor can access corporate systems or data, it must be assessed, approved, and regularly reviewed. In a large organization with thousands of vendors, that means endless questionnaires, certificates, trust centers, spreadsheets, and email follow-ups—all of which become outdated and must be collected again.

This operational fatigue creates a security problem. Every vendor connection can become a path into the organization, while an annual assessment provides only a snapshot. A vendor may be trustworthy when approved and breached months later, still holding access to critical systems and sensitive data.

Shift rebuilds this operation around AI agents. The agents collect evidence, read security documents, chase missing answers, assess controls, maintain reviews, and continuously monitor vendors for changes and breaches. When a security event occurs, Shift connects it to the organization’s own context—what the vendor can access, which assets may be affected, and how critical the relationship is—so the SOC can understand the real exposure and respond.

This creates a new division of labor. AI carries the continuous and repetitive operation. The vendor-risk team decides whether trust should be granted or maintained. The SOC decides whether access must be restricted or contained when that trust changes. Humans remain responsible for the consequential decisions, with the evidence and context needed to make them.

As Founding Designer, my role is to shape this relationship between people and autonomous agents: how the system explains its work, communicates uncertainty, asks for judgment, and allows people to intervene. We are not adding AI to an old workflow—we are turning third-party risk from a periodic administrative process into a continuously operating security defense.
