Most organizations don’t lack security tools. They have firewalls, endpoint protection, email security, identity systems, and many other products from different vendors. The problem is that no team can continuously configure every product against every vulnerability and emerging attack.

A security professional may understand the threat but not know the exact control that should be changed in every affected product. As the stack grows, so does the distance between the protection an organization has purchased and the protection it is actually receiving.

Veriti was built around a different assumption: instead of adding another defensive layer, strengthen the layers already inside the organization. The platform monitored the existing security stack, identified where its configuration left the organization exposed, and translated each threat into specific actions—what should change, in which product, and why.

The ambition was to raise every part of the wall to the height it was capable of reaching. Veriti could recommend the required configuration and, when approved, apply the remediation across the security stack with a single action.

As Veriti’s founding designer, my challenge was to make those recommendations understandable and trustworthy. Changing a security control can affect production systems, so “one click” could never mean “blind action.” The product needed to explain the threat, reveal the configuration gap, identify the affected products, and show what would happen before anything changed.

This shaped the entire experience: how we represented security insights, how teams prioritized a large volume of findings, how we connected one threat to several products, and how we turned a recommendation into controlled remediation.

Veriti wasn’t another dashboard describing how exposed an organization was. It was designed to close the gap—from knowing that a weakness exists to configuring the existing defenses to stop it.
